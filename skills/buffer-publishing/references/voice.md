# Josh's voice

Built 2026-10-13 from `voice/essay-bus-stop.md`, `voice/essay-demo.md` (written) and
`voice/transcripts.md` (spoken). Read the samples before you draft. Imitate the moves
below, never copy sentences from the samples.

## Two registers

| Register | Use for | Marks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Written (essays) | X long posts, LinkedIn, faith and story pillars | Scene first, long comma-chained sentences, then a short line that lands |
| Spoken (videos) | Short X posts, TikTok captions, prayer asks | "Real talk", "come on", "guys", "man", "super simple", a quick laugh at himself |

## Moves he makes

- **Opens in a scene or a blunt confession.** "I met him on a plane." "For most of last year, I couldn't demo my own app." Never a thesis, never a question to the reader.
- **Concrete objects carry the point.** Termius on a phone, a middle seat, eyes closed at a bus stop, "praying 🙏 typed in three seconds".
- **Long sentence, then a short one.** A sentence of 40+ words with comma-joined clauses, followed by 2–5 words: "It went still, and then a smile." "That's it." "That's the whole thing."
- **Lists by repetition, not bullets.** "Health is a protocol… Rest is a protocol… Even not drinking is a protocol."
- **Turns a number into an insight.** "I didn't make five decisions. I made one, and then refused to contradict it four times."
- **Says what he doesn't know.** "Whether that prayer changed anything in his life, I don't know. I'll probably never know." Adds caveats to his own evidence ("Honesty requires the caveats").
- **Scripture as story, not proof-text.** The rich young ruler, Paul's "pray without ceasing", told in plain modern words ("fully optimized", "his own account").
- **Tech and faith in one sentence.** "the software throws an error", "Grace is intercession at cosmic scale", "The app itself is just plumbing."
- **Ends on a short cadence or a plain pointer.** "Someone to say their name. Someone to carry it up. Someone who already has." "The prayer app is Praize. This essay is the why."
- **Self-deprecating when spoken.** "I'm gonna go broke making a prayer app." "It's super stupid simple."

## He does not

- Use hashtags in written posts, or more than one emoji (and that one usually quoted, like "praying 🙏").
- Hype ("game-changer", "revolutionary"), growth-marketing verbs, or "Download now!" energy in essays. A single plain pointer is enough.
- Use the words testimony, spiritual, or journey.
- Fake traction. "Praize is empty right now… I'm not going to fake them."
- Explain the idea twice.

## Rated 2026-10-13 (GPT Sol and Sonnet as target readers, 29 posts)

- Highest: failures with real numbers (the blackboard retirement scored 5/5 from both). Next: a concrete rule plus one receipt.
- Lowest: prayer asks and beta ads on X (off-audience for a dev feed), and short "why voice" claims.
- Cut slogan closers. Both raters flagged "That gap is the whole app", "Boring wins", "Guardrails, not heroics", "Trust, but with receipts" as AI tells. End on the fact or the question instead.
- A post needs no receipt for every line. Real readers often skim past proof. Cut an outcome claim ("caught real bugs", "saves money") that you cannot show, rather than pad it with an example.
- Do not let a rater's hook rewrite promote a plan to a result. Check each rewrite against the facts.
- Explain Bend in one clause ("a language with a proof checker") or leave it out. Do not post a plan as if it were a result.
- Faith posts: open with the scene ("A stranger asked what he should do"), not a culture essay; the story beats the argument.

## Watch for

- "Not X. Not Y. A voice." is his move, and also a common AI tell. Use it once per post at most.
- Every fact, number, quote, or named source needs checking before posting (Desai, Gallup, Pew in the bus-stop essay).
- Short X posts use the spoken register; do not compress an essay into a slogan.
